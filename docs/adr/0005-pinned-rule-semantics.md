# Pinned Rule semantics before the first act is scored

The engine review of November 2026 (`docs/reviews/engine-review-2026-11.md`) found that several Rule semantics were either undecided or decided by accident, and that none of the surprises raised an error. Every act will be scored and authored against these semantics, so they are pinned here before Phase 6 starts. Each decision is enforced by the engine and asserted in `qa:core`, `qa:stress` (section "pinned"), `qa:prove` and `qa:turn`.

1. **The opening counts as entering an act.** Starting an Investigation is a turn: `InvestigationStarted`, the Clues of any Fact the player starts knowing, then the cascade that entering the opening location sets off. `enter` Rules on the opening location fire, and so do arrival Rules for everyone standing there. Before this, the opening ran no Rules, while every later act entry fired `enter` on its anchor.

2. **Proximity fires on arrival, not on every turn.** A `proximity` Rule fires when the player and the person come to share a location: the player walks in, a Rule moves or summons the person, a schedule brings them at a period change, an act entry puts them together, or the game opens with them together. It does not fire again while they stay together. The firing happens in the turn that caused the arrival, so its Effects are visible before the player types the next command. Two consequences an author must know: a `proximity` Rule whose `when` only comes true while the two stay together (a flag set, a Fact learned) does not fire until they part and meet again, so that scene belongs on the Trigger that makes the condition true; and an arrival is one link in a cascade, so an arrival caused at the eighth depth is cut like any other ninth link.

3. **An act boundary is time enough for every rumour.** Entering an act delivers every delayed `grantFact` still in flight, emitted as `FactLearned` right after `ActEntered`. The engine cannot tell whether the next act starts an hour or a week later, since `dateLabel` is free text. A rumour meant to land partway through the next act is an ordinary Rule gated on that act. Two smaller rules go with this: a `clock` Rule set exactly at an act's start time fails validation, because the clock starts there and never passes it; and a Tick that enters an act adds no Tick minutes, as a player turn already did not.

4. **A `leave` Rule judges its conditions from the room being left.** Its `when` sees the world as it is, with the player still in the location being left, so `location` and `personsPresent` mean what an author expects. Every other trigger sees the world after the action. Firing order is the action, then `leave`, then `enter`, then arrivals, then whatever those set off; Story order applies only among the Rules of one firing.

5. **The prover proves only what a player can select.** Every prover action passes the same Candidate Set check the turn endpoint applies (`inCandidateSet`, now in `packages/schema/candidates.ts`). The endpoint first settles the Selection (`trimSelection`): a stray word is dropped, a real id is moved to the one empty slot that takes it, a candidate's exact name stands for its id, `open` or `use` on an exit itself, or the exit's own key used on it, is a `move` through it, and `examine` with an object as secondary ("look at the ship through the telescope") is that object used on the target, while `read` keeps reading; an ask's topic that is not one of the set's topics is dropped (the designed deflection). Each of these rewrites to a Selection with the same effect as one the prover offers, so the two agree. Two consequences for what a player can select:
   - A deduction may accuse anyone the player has met, present or not. The Candidate Set carries them as `suspects`, by the label the player knows them by, and they are exactly the people the diary lists as met. An absent person a turn names reaches the narrator by that label too (`personsNamed`), never as a bare id.
   - `examine` may target a person present, and `use` may take a person present as its secondary.

   The prover can also always wait until the next period, so content gated by a period (a room, a schedule, a delayed grant) is reachable whether or not some `clock` Rule happens to exist. "wait until X" in a walkthrough stands for as many waits as it takes.

6. **`visibleFromAct` gates speech, not learning.** It is the earliest act in which a person will tell the player the Fact when asked. A document or a Rule may teach it earlier, including a Rule that has a person tell it (§9); the prover reports the first such path it meets as EARLY, and the validator warns on a `grantFact` that teaches the player a Fact (given to the player, told to them, or told by them) and can fire earlier, naming the teller. The EARLY list is a net, not a proof of absence: the search stops once every goal is found, so a leak only reachable deeper than that is not seen. Granting it early to a person is not reported: they still cannot tell it before its act.

7. **`knownBy: ["player"]` is honoured.** The player starts knowing the Fact, may name it in a deduction or tell it to others, and finds its Clue on the opening turn.

8. **A `combine` Rule also fires when one of its pair is used on the other.** `Combined` and a `Used` whose `on` is an Object both raise the combine Firing, after the action's own; the prover offers both. Neither verb pairs an Object with itself: `use` and `combine` both refuse it, and `trimSelection` reads a `use` whose secondary repeats its target as the plain `use`. The validator warns where an action Rule on that use claims the same pair, since both then land in one turn.

9. **A Rule can have a person tell a Fact (`grantFact` with `from`).** Added so a Rule whose Beats have a person speak can put on record that they said it. It is recorded as an answered `ask`: the teller learns the Fact first if they did not know it, then `Spoke` and `FactDisclosed`, so the Ledger, the diary's Persons, who hears the turn, the Clue and `disclosed` Rules all see a person telling it. The player is always on one side: the validator fails a grant between two people, since the Ledger records only exchanges with the player. As with an ask, a person tells the player even what the player already knows, and the player tells a person only what they do not. The other side must be present: if they are not, the grant is a plain unattributed `grantFact`, and the validator warns unless the Rule's `when.personsPresent` names them. A delayed grant cannot have a teller.

10. **Telling is not asking.** A Fact the player knows and a person present does not is offered to them as a "tell" topic. The selector says whether a line tells (`telling` on the ask: a claim, an answer, the player's own view) or asks; a "tell" topic is accepted only on a telling line, and a question about that subject is an ask with no topic, the designed deflection. This keeps a question from committing something the player has not said. And a person never tells the player back what the player told them: once a Fact is in their Ledger as heard, it is not offered to the player as something to ask them, though they still know it and Rules still see that they do.

## Considered options

- **Proximity at the start of every turn (the previous behaviour).** Rejected: a greeting lands one turn late, a repeatable proximity Rule fires on every turn, and a proximity Rule that changes the world (enters an act, dismisses someone) does so after the player has chosen a command against the old world.
- **Count the period boundaries an act jump skips, as if the clock had run forward.** Rejected: the engine does not know how much time passes between acts, so the result would depend on whether the next act starts earlier or later in the day, which is not what an author means.
- **Evaluate `leave` against a frozen copy of the pre-move State.** Rejected: two `leave` Rules on one exit could no longer exclude each other with `flagsNone`. Judging from the room left, with flags live, keeps that pattern working.
- **Suspects must be present.** Rejected: every accusation scene would have to bring the suspect on stage.
- **`visibleFromAct` as a hard gate on all learning.** Rejected: it would forbid the deliberate early clue, a letter read before anyone will talk about it. The prover's EARLY report catches the accidental one.
- **Fail a `clock` Rule at any time before its act's start.** Rejected: overnight Rules (midnight bells in an evening act) are normal. The validator fails only a Rule restricted to an act (by `trigger.act` or `when.act`) at that act's exact start time, and warns when its first firing would come more than twelve hours after the act starts. An unrestricted daily Rule at some act's start time only warns.

## Consequences

- Saves from before this change replay to a different State (a greeting moves from turn 1 to the opening, rumours mature at act entry). Start a fresh Investigation.
- `qa:turn` recordings were re-authored, since the opening narration context and the Candidate Set changed.
- A score's **Trigger** column for `proximity` describes an arrival. Ambient chatter while someone stays present is a `tick` Rule with `personsPresent`.
- What remains of the review is independent of these decisions and can be fixed in any order: the invention gate, the endpoint's handling of disconnects and double gate rejections, the `events` line sent to the browser, the validator gaps, and the cost of loading the log.
